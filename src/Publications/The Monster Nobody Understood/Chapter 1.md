It had been forty-five minutes since ten, and he still hadn't shown up. I checked my watch again. 10:47.

The glass partition reflected the pale fluorescent white lights above me, buzzing faintly. The kind of buzz you only notice once everything else goes quite. There was a faint smell of disinfectant too, maybe it was the glass pane. Beyond it was another small section, just as empty as the one I was sitting in, only  half a table and a metal chair. That section was separated from mine by only the inch-thick pane. Apparently it was supposed to protect me in case something goes wrong. But I think both the warden and I knew that in case anything really goes wrong, the glass won't do much.

 I wondered whether he was even aware that I was waiting. The guards hadn't said much. In fact, I don't think either of them had spoken a word to me since I arrived. I tried to peek through the window on the door to see if I could notice either of them, but the window proved too small.

Somewhere down the corridor a door clanged shut. Footsteps faded. Then nothing but the hum of the lights.

I shifted in my chair, trying to find a more comfortable position. It wasn't working. I looked at the folder resting on my lap. Twenty years' worth of records, interviews, evaluations, and research. I'd spent months going through it all, and yet, sitting there, I found myself wondering whether any of it would actually be useful.

Just as I went to ask my watch for answers it couldn't give anyway, the door finally beeped open. He was the first to walk in. I knew he was a big man, but I hadn't imagined how imposing he would be in real life.

The chains at his ankles clinked with each step, making a dull metallic rhythm on the concrete. Two men, just slightly shorter than him followed in shortly. They were wearing different uniforms than the other guards. They took him to the chair opposite me. He sat down with a thud on the chair like a child, making a grunting noise only an old man makes.

Must be hard having to walk around with all that weight from the cuffs and chains. And they can't open them for this interview either. I don't think they would be comfortable with having his hands and legs be so... free.

But they proceeded to open his handcuffs anyways, leaving only the legs tied. They promptly left the room after that. Leaving the two of us in the small space, divided only by that glass partition.

He hadn't paid much attention to me yet, not even a look. He stretched his hands while still seated in the same chair I was in. But he seemed to have no discomfort with it whatsoever.

When he was done, only then, he finally looked towards me. I thought for a moment he would speak first. But I knew better than that and I was only met with a blank, unreadable stare. It didn't feel particularly hostile, but rather annoyed perhaps.

"Hello, Mr Wieherer. How are you doing today?" I had practiced that line so many times in my head before this, and yet it still came out weird.

"Wouldn't you like to know." He replied, a flat, monotonous voice, with a hint of sarcasm.

"Well, as a matter of fact, very much so, yes." I let out an awkward laugh, fully aware that I had already lost any meaningful control of this conversation with that horrid sentence. "Um, I am sorry, I am getting ahead of myself." I pulled up the folder from my lap and placed it on the table. Flipping through the first few pages to find my notes.

"My name is Fieg Ginnerman. My team and I are here to understand you, Mr. Wieherer."

"Understand me?" He said, in an almost worrying voice. "And how are you going to do that, Dr. Fieg?" He tilted his head slightly, almost mocking me with his question.

"Well, I am glad I've piqued your interest, Mr Wieherer, because we are here to understand _why_ you think the way you do." That was my main hook. Give him the impression that this time it was more about _him_, and not just another attempt at trying to figure out why he did it.

I thought I could get a reaction out of him with that, but he still only replied with his cold stare. I almost thought I saw a flicker of a quick smile on his face before he spoke, "Ah, now isn't that the question." He paused to look at the white ceiling above us for a moment before snapping his gaze back on me, "why does anyone think the way they do, Dr. Fieg?"

He watched intently as I tried to come up with an answer. "People's experiences, surroundings, relationships, and their individual ways of interpreting things all contribute to how they thi-

"That's a rather broad answer, isn't it, Dr. Fieg?" I knew he was toying with me at this point.

"Have you ever wondered why you think the way you do?" I asked, only a half-decent attempt to dodge the question.

"What do you think?" He replied with that same uninterested tone.

"I don't know, that's why I am here to try and find out." I smiled for a brief moment; an unassuming habit of humans to ridicule their own lapse, though not the most suited for this situation.

He didn't reply, but I couldn't let go now, "So, what usually occupies your day, Mr. Wieherer?"

"Nothing much." He paused to take a long breath, still never taking his eyes off me, "there's usually not much to do."

"Do you enjoy having visitors?"

"No."

"Then I imagine I'm not making a very good first impression." I said, as I desperately tried to awkwardly laugh it off.

"You aren't." He said, but this time with a hint of something resembling a faint smile. But he wiped it off too quickly. "But at least you're better than the others." He continued.

"Others?"

"People who've come here before you." He took another breath, but this time it was almost a sigh, "the ones who said they wanted to 'understand how I think'." He let a small smile slip through on his left cheek. Was he perhaps testing me?

"And what made those previous encounters unpleasant? Perhaps I can learn something from them?" I said, in a joyful tone.

He studied my face for a moment before letting out a dry chuckle.

"You have clearly done your research," he said, pointing towards the open folder on the table between us, "you know what made those previous encounters," he took a sharp breath, "'unpleasant'." His looked at me with a serious intent as he drew another long breath. But then he let out a small smile, something that seemed almost genuine, "They wanted me to tell them something about myself." He looked at the folder again, and the smile vanished into a serious face, "something they could write down." His gaze slowly lifted up to meet my eyes.

I closed the folder and placed my hands on top of it. I didn't want to simply annoy him into leaving. Not after him just starting to open up.

"So what _did_ you want to tell them?" I leaned forward slightly. Closer to the glass, the air between us felt thick. I could almost sense his heat from the other side of the glass, or so I imagined. More likely it was my own nervousness, being that close to him.

He looked at me with an almost disappointed look. For a moment it felt as though he might break through glass. But he didn't. Neither did he reply. He simply got up from his chair, scraping it briefly against the floor, and walked up to the door. The guards outside slid open a small opening in the door with a metallic scrape. He put his hands through it and soon they were cuffed again.

The door beeped open, the sound sharp in the small room, and they took him out.

I was disappointed that I had let go of such a good opportunity. He'd just opened up. But I was also only slightly relieved. I took a big breath as I closed eyes, trying to comprehend what even just occurred in these last 30 minutes.

As I finally got up from the chair and started collecting my folder and coat, the door beeped open. The two guards from earlier and my colleagues were standing outside.

"Congratulations, Dr. Fieg." Charles said, grinning as though the whole thing had been a sport.

"For what?" I couldn't laugh, nor smile. That was nothing to be congratulated about. I completely ruined the interview. He was just opening up. I could have gotten him to talk more. If only I didn't bring up-

"For surviving your first interview with that monster, doctor." Hugh added, his voice low and careful.

"Yeah," I let out a dry chuckle, but deep down, I knew. There was nothing funny about it.

As the guards escorted us back through the long, narrow hallways towards our quarters, I couldn't help but wonder, why did he leave so abruptly? Was it really about the previous researchers? Did he think I was trying to do the same thing? Or was it something else that made him leave?

I kept replaying the last exchange. He had smiled when he mentioned the previous researchers, almost warmly, and then he left without another word. I thought he was beginning to open up. That smile suggested he was. But the departure said otherwise.

Did I pushed too far? Perhaps I should have left the question alone. Or maybe I misunderstood what he meant when he said the others wanted something they could write down. Or did he actually wanted to tell me something, and I ruined the chance by asking? What did Wieherer actually want to tell the previous researchers?
